Good questions.
Clear answers.

Is this KYC, a background check, or both?

Vetra organizes identity verification and a candidate screening workflow. KYC methods can support identity checks; employment screening also needs authorized sources, candidate rights and a process suited to the hiring location. The pilot supports manual evidence review and an identity integration that requires activation. Criminal history and right-to-work providers are not connected.

How does this relate to Maigret’s username search?

Username discovery is a separate investigative capability. A username match does not establish identity, employment history or suitability for a role. Vetra’s hiring workflow does not use public profile discovery as a verified identity result or an automated hiring signal.

What can candidates see and control?

The invitation opens a candidate portal with the requested checks and a consent step. Candidates can provide statements, see check status, request a correction and withdraw consent. Withdrawal stops the workflow, revokes the invitation and removes report details; any connected provider cleanup still needs confirmation and follow-up.

Should we build this workflow or buy a screening service?

Use a screening provider for verified data and checks you are not equipped to conduct. Vetra is a workflow layer for managing candidate consent, evidence and reviewer actions. Teams with immediate multi-country screening, service-level or certification requirements should procure those capabilities from an established provider while assessing Vetra separately.

Is the pilot ready for enterprise production?

It is a working pilot foundation. Production use still requires a security review, operational controls, appropriate provider agreements and legal review for the intended market. Enterprise requirements such as SSO, independently assessed security and contractual service levels need further implementation and validation.
